Important points in history

  • All age groups
  • History of animal contact, e.g. Bite, Scratch, Claws
  • Animal's behaviour (?sick, ?tests by veterinarian)
  • Over wounds site:
    • Pain
    • Pruritis
    • Paraesthesia
  • Fever
  • Malaise
  • Headache
  • Vomiting
  • Anorexia
  • Symptoms of autonomic instability e.g. unstable temperature, sweaty, piloerection, priapism

Important points on examination

  • ABC
  • BCS
  • Seizures/ agitation
  • Aerophobia
  • Hydration status
  • Neuro-flaccidity, hyporeflexia
  • +/- hypotension

Investigations

  • Water test if necessary but this can be very distressing.
  • Fan test (patients have severe aerophobia)
  • At post mortem, a supra orbital brain biopsy can be performed

Differential Diagnosis

  • Meningitis
  • Tetanus
  • Cerebral Malaria
  • GBS for paralytic rabies
  • Other viral encephalitis

Management

Establish what category of exposure. Per the WHO guidelines an exposure is any of the following, from any mammal – but especially from dogs:

Post exposure prophylaxis (immediately after a (possibly) rabid dog bite)

  • Local wound care - must be washed for 15 minutes with water and iodine (or soap if iodine is not available) and then cleaned with antiseptic (in case of a mucus membrane exposure flush the area with copious amounts of clean water). Do not suture if there is an open wound.

  • The patient should get a certificate from the Government Veterinary Laboratory. This will allow them to get free rabies post-exposure vaccines from the DHO pharmacy and Gateway clinic.
  • If the animal is suspected of being rabid call the Mission Rabies Hotline on 088531992 / 0992566022

Antibiotics

(e.g. Augmentin: 22.5 mg/kg per dose (amoxicillin component) BD (maximum 875 mg amoxicillin and 125 mg clavulanate per dose)
plus
Metronidazole: 10 mg/kg per dose TDS (maximum 500 mg per dose))

Vaccines

  • TTV see Immunisation protocol

  • Give the Anti-rabies (chick embryo) vaccine at days 0, 3, 7, 14, 30 and 90
  • It is given IM into the deltoid muscle,
  • The first dose is given at the nearest health unit & subsequent doses by the DHO
  • Interaction of vaccine: with IM antimalarials, immunosuppressive drugs & steroids
  • Complications of the vaccine: include local reactions e.g. urticarial rash and general
    • reactions e.g. anaphylaxis, non specific myalgia, headaches, dizziness, nausea and vomiting
  • The vaccine has occasionally caused a post rabies vaccine encephalomyelitis (although this is very rare with chick or duck embryo derived vaccine)
  • The vaccine in QECH is available on request from the pharmacy

Consider also

  • Have any other children been bitten? If so, they will also require vaccination
  • Consider vaccines for those exposed due to occupational and recreational reasons
  • Has the offending dog/animal been seen by a vetinary surgeon. If it is a stray dog assume dog is rabid until proved otherwise.

Management of clinical cases of Rabies ENCEPHALITIS

Treatment is supportive.

  • Sedation
    • Diazepam and/or Phenobarbitone -> Infuse in an IV drip. Ask a senior for advice
    • +/- Analgesics
    • Consider chloralhydrate. Via NGT
  • IV fluids - insert IV as soon as safely possible. Patients frequently complain of dehydration in lucid periods.
  • Broad spectrum antibiotics cover if patient febrile/ critically unwell/ doubt over diagnosis
  • Manage in a side-room/ quiet area.
  • Counsel the family - involve Palliative care
