Meningitis

Does not include:
  • Cryptococcal meningitis
  • TB Meningitis

When to suspect Meningitis

  • In any child with fever and meningism (vomiting, neck stiffness, headache, photophobia, positive kernig's and brudzinski's signs, etc)
  • In any infant presenting with fever, poor feeding, bulging fontanelle or irritability
  • In any infant/ child with fever and convulsions (other than simple febrile convulsions) with no other obvious cause found.
  • In a child in whom it is difficult to rule out meningitis, especially in young infants and neonates who may present with subtle, non-specific signs e.g. not feeding, lethargy, irritability, jaundice or apnoeic episodes.

  • Do an LP unless in severe respiratory/ cardiac failure, evidence of a bleeding disorder, infection of the overlying skin or any life threatening condition likely to be complicated by the procedure.

  • Antibiotics should be given as an EMERGENCY. Treatment should not be delayed for LP results. If feasible, do LP before starting antibiotics, but if LP is not possible right away, start antibiotics empirically and consider doing LP later. Pre-LP antibiotics will reduce the chances for bacterial growth but will not immidiately influence white blood cells count (WBC) count and chemistry.

Treatment

Resuscitation

  • ABC
  • Correct shock and dehydration
  • Correct hypoglycaemia
  • Stop seizures ( Convulsion protocol)

Antibiotics

  • Health centre: follow IMCI guidelines "Very Severe Febrile Disease"
  • In Hospital

INFANTS (> 2months of age) AND CHILDREN

Ceftriaxone 100mg/ kg/ o.d IV/ IM x 7 - 10 days

NEONATES and INFANTS <2 month of age

X-pen 100, 000 IU/ kg/ dose 6-hourly (QID)
+ Gentamycin 6mg/ kg OD
for 14 - 21 days

OR

Ceftriaxone 100 mg/ kg OD for 10 days
(discuss with Consultant)

  • Try to keep the IV treatment for at least 48 hours AND as long as possible; but IM antibiotics can then be used
  • Give all antibiotics parenterally
  • Switch to appropriate antibiotics as soon as culture & sensitivities are known
  • Steroids are NOT necessary

    • Salmonella Meningitis

    • Salmonella meningitis should be treated for at least 14 days with ceftriaxone followed by Ciprofloxacin 10mg/ kg/ dose/ BD PO for 14 days (on discharge)

    Supportive Management

    Fluids/ Nutritional Support

    • Give maintenance fluids once shock and dehydration have been corrected (IV initially or later by NGT), unless taking fluid well by mouth
    • Avoid over hydration by careful fluid balance and in particular avoid IV fluids with low sodium levels e. g. 5% dextrose
    • Use 0.9% normal saline or Ringer's lactate with added glucose to make 10% dextrose for infants and those children with hypoglycaemia ( Fluids protocol))
    • If still unable to tolerate fluids by mouth after 36 - 48 hours
      1. pass a nasogastric tube (NGT)
      2. give boluses of milk/ sugar solution

      Blood Sugar

      • Check blood glucose
        1. on all children on presentation
        2. on any child who starts fitting
        3. on any child with deteriorating conscious level

      • If blood sugar < 2.5 mmol/ l
        1. give 1ml/kg 50% dextrose iv stat slowly (ideally dilute with Ringer's or saline at least 1:1). Or use 2 ml/kg 25% dextrose or 5 ml/kg of 10% dextrose
        2. Continue maintenance IV fluids with added dextrose
        3. consider NGT feeds

      • Recheck blood glucose after 30 minutes. If it remains low, repeat the IV glucose and continue maintenance fluids and/or NGT feeds and monitoring

      Oxygen

      • Give when necessary e.g. when SaO2 < 90%, child cyanosed, or in respiratory distress

      Hyperpyrexia

      • Most children will need paracetamol and/or ibuprofen for fever and pain relief
        1. Give 30mg/ kg as PR loading dose
        2. or 20mg/ kg as PO loading dose
        3. and then 15mg/ kg as maintenance doses up to four times daily (QID)

      Convulsions

      Convulsion protocol

      Monitoring

      • Follow the 'general' CCP
      • If there is no improvement after 3rd day, e.g. fever, focal neurological signs or deteriorating coma scale, exclude obvious causes:
        1. US Scan (subdural effusion or an abscess)
        2. Repeat LP
        3. Check CSF cultures (is the organism resistant?)
      • Look for other foci of infection
        1. cellulitis at injection sites
        2. arthritis
        3. osteomyelitis
      • Check if treatment is being given (especially night doses); correct dose?
      • If fever persists beyond 10 - 14 days, or there is a history of TB contact, TB meningitis should be considered

      Discharge and Follow - Up

      • Full neurological exam before discharge
      • Pay attention to cranial nerve deficits, motor deficits
      • All children should have a hearing test Audiology and head circumference recorded in health passport books, follow up measurements at 1 month and 6 months
      • Follow up Ultrasound Scans may be necessary for abnormally increasing head circumference (ventricular/ subdural collections).
      • If neurological deficit found refer to physiotherapists ( Physiotherapy)
      • If persistent seizures refer to general paediatric or paediatric neurology clinic
      • If deaf refer to Ear Nose and Throat clinic at QECH or Educational Centre for the deaf at Nguludi (write a referral note in the health passport)
